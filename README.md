# CS-360
Coursework for SNHU CS-360

Briefly summarize the requirements and goals of the app you developed. What user needs was this app designed to address?

This app is called Weight Tracking. It is a minimalistic weight tracking application with 3 databases and 4 main pages. There is one user login database, one weight and date database, and one goal weight database. It features a login page, a main page that displays the weight history as a grid, a settings page to update SMS permissions for notifications, and a page to update the goal weight of the user. This app will allow a user to track their weight for the purpose of gaining, losing, or maintaining their weight.


What screens and features were necessary to support user needs and produce a user-centered UI for the app? How did your UI designs keep users in mind? Why were your designs successful?

When entering the app, you first hit a login screen where you can log in or create a user login. This retains the security of the users personal information. After a successful login, the user is taken to the main page, where they can view their weight history, add new records, navigate to their settings, or change their weight goal. The UI design is intended to make the interface easy to use and uncluttered to make it intuitive for the user. I considered the user experience on every page and made sure the flow of information was logical throughout the design process.


How did you approach the process of coding your app? What techniques or strategies did you use? How could those techniques or strategies be applied in the future?

When coding the app, I followed a flexible, iterative process that allowed on-the-fly problem-solving while following best practice recommendations. Because I designed the UI first, I had a plan for the flow of information and the different segments of the app ahead of time. I first gathered the requirements based on the assignment, built the databases, and then built the flow of the app. Once all code was built to the point that the app was functional, I began testing and adjusting the functionality based on the testing results.


How did you test to ensure your code was functional? Why is this process important, and what did it reveal?
I tested the app using the simulator to ensure each process worked as intended and when I found flaws in the logic or errors, I addressed them. I then would test using the simulator again to find additional flaws and ensure the fixes I created were working as intended.


Consider the full app design and development process from initial planning to finalization. Where did you have to innovate to overcome a challenge?

To meet the requirements of the app, a lot of research and problem-solving was required. The area where I had the most challenges was the functionality of the goal weight. The way I had originally wanted it to work wouldn't work for me. This required me to pivot and plan new functionality that still met the requirements. 


In what specific component of your mobile app were you particularly successful in demonstrating your knowledge, skills, and experience?

The component that went the smoothest for me and that I was most confident about being successful was the login and account creation functionality. This was a process that I already understood and had a good plan for. It did not require any extra problem-solving due to this.
